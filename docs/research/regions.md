# Regional countryside reference

Representative motifs for the village and forest band between tracks. Sizes below are **width × height in pixels**, including roofs and crowns. Hex colors and pixel simplifications are suggested art palettes, not measured historical colors. Regional traditions vary; keep the wetland, dryland, and mountain settings identified below separate when composing a scene.

## 1. Kansai, Japan (Osaka)

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Minka farmhouse, 15 × 11:** low timber-and-plaster body; steep thatched roof with hipped ends, a short ridge, and broad eaves. Walls `#D8CDB5`, exposed timber `#604A38`, weathered thatch `#89734E`. Distinctive detail: a dark, projecting eave shading the doorway. Sennan and Miyama provide local references. |
| **Trees and plants — mountain cherry** | **Yamazakura, 11 × 12:** branching brown trunk `#715346`, loose rounded crown `#648650`. Pale blossom `#F3DDE1` mixed with reddish young leaves `#A46E54` in spring; bare branches in winter. Native Japanese cherry, prominent around Yoshino in Nara. |
| **Trees and plants — Japanese red pine** | **Pinus densiflora, 10 × 15:** slender, sometimes leaning trunk `#A36B4B`; irregular horizontal foliage pads `#365B43`, with gaps between tiers. Evergreen; avoid making every mature pine a solid triangle. |
| **Trees and plants — Japanese maple** | **Acer palmatum, 11 × 10:** short forked trunk `#756052`, broad layered crown with a jagged edge `#65804B`. Autumn crown `#BE533D` with `#DA9049` highlights; winter silhouette bare. Native to Japan. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **Shrine torii, 9 × 10:** two uprights, one straight crossbar, and a longer top lintel with slightly raised ends; vermilion `#B64E36`, dark cap `#443F39`. Place at a shrine approach. |

**Terrain:** rice-growing valley floors and wooded foothills, with layered mountains behind the villages.

**Sources:** [Osaka Convention & Tourism Bureau — Former Mukai Residence](https://osaka-info.jp/en/spot/former-residence-mukui-family/); [Miyama Tourism Association — Thatched-Roof Village](https://miyamanavi.com/en/sightseeing/detail/id%3D362); [Government of Japan — native cherries and Yoshino](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/202104/202104_01_en.html); [Hokkaido University — Japanese red pine](https://hosho.ees.hokudai.ac.jp/tsuyu/top/plt/pine/pinus/den.html) and [Japanese maple](https://hosho.ees.hokudai.ac.jp/tsuyu/top/plt/maple/acer/pal.html); [Japan National Tourism Organization — shrine traditions](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/shrine-and-temple-traditions/).

## 2. Jalisco, Mexico (Guadalajara)

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Adobe village house, 13 × 8:** squat rectangular body and a shallow-to-moderate gable of clay tiles. Earth/plaster walls `#CEB08A`, roof `#A85E43`, wooden door `#674A36`. Distinctive detail: a one-pixel projecting eave. Adobe, sometimes mixed with stone, and tiled timber roofs are documented local traditions. |
| **Trees and plants — blue agave** | **Agave tequilana, 9 × 6:** stem concealed beneath a basal rosette of stiff, pointed leaves; blue-gray `#789B9B`, shadow `#4D7376`. Cultivated regional emblem; repeat in spaced rows. Keep the field sprite as a rosette rather than a tree. |
| **Trees and plants — jacaranda** | **Jacaranda mimosifolia, 12 × 13:** slim forked trunk `#705344`, broad, airy crown `#63854C`. Violet blossom masses `#9B7AC0` in late winter/spring. Introduced from South America; use near settlements and gardens. |
| **Trees and plants — mesquite** | **Prosopis laevigata, 12 × 10:** dark crooked trunk `#65503B`, spreading branches and an open, flattened crown `#80834C`. Native dryland tree; pale yellow flower flecks `#D7C878` in spring. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **Tequila volcano, 13 × 7 background motif:** broad volcanic mass with an irregular summit, muted `#777B87` and shaded flank `#606A78`; use behind the agave fields rather than among the houses. |

**Terrain:** inland valleys, reddish agricultural soils, rolling uplands, and volcanic mountains; the Guadalajara setting is not coastal.

**Sources:** [Jalisco Culture Secretariat — traditional adobe architecture](https://sc.jalisco.gob.mx/sites/sc.jalisco.gob.mx/files/14entreadobesyt.pdf); [UNESCO — Agave Landscape and Ancient Industrial Facilities of Tequila](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1209/); [UNAM Iztacala Botanical Garden — blue agave](https://sitios.iztacala.unam.mx/jabiz/agave-tequilana/); [Guadalajara metropolitan tree manual](https://peim-arbolado.semaqroo.gob.mx/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Manual_arbolado_ver1.5.pdf); [ITESO university — mesquite](https://iteso.mx/web/general/detalle?group_id=19270647).

## 3. Valley of Mexico (Mexico City)

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Milpa Alta vernacular house, 12 × 9:** rectangular volcanic-stone body under a moderate gable of flat clay tiles. Walls `#79736B`, mortar `#B6AC98`, roof `#A76049`. Distinctive detail: one deeply recessed wooden door `#574637`. This is a southern highland reference, not a universal basin house. |
| **Trees and plants — chinampa willow** | **Ahuejote, Salix bonplandiana, 5 × 16:** narrow upright crown `#64844D` on a straight slender trunk `#78654D`; keep branches ascending rather than broadly weeping. Native; planted along chinampa edges. |
| **Trees and plants — Montezuma cypress** | **Ahuehuete, Taxodium mucronatum, 13 × 15:** thick, flared trunk `#796451`, broad irregular crown with soft hanging tips `#58744B`. Native waterside tree; foliage may thin in cold or dry conditions. |
| **Trees and plants — nopal** | **Cultivated Opuntia spp., 7 × 8:** short woody base `#796747`, branching chains of upright oval pads `#6D9255`, darker joints `#4E7445`. Emblematic crop around Milpa Alta; use on dry fields, away from the canal edge. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **Chinampa plot and canal, 15 × 6:** low rectangular raised bed `#876749`, green crop strip `#70914C`, blue-gray channel `#658D97`, and a few upright edge stakes `#68533D`. These are fixed raised fields, not drifting rafts; willow sprites can mark their margins. |

**Terrain:** a high enclosed basin ringed by volcanic ranges; southern wetlands and canals contrast with cultivated upland slopes.

**Sources:** [UNAM field research in Revista INVI — rural housing in Milpa Alta](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-83582011000300007&script=sci_arttext); [FAO — Chinampas Agricultural System in Mexico City](https://www.fao.org/giahs/giahs-around-the-world/mexico-chinampas-agricultural-system/20th-anniversary-celebrations-of-the-globally-important-agricultural-heritage-systems-%28giahs%29-programme/en); [UNAM eFloraMex — Salix bonplandiana](https://efloramex.ib.unam.mx/cdm_dataportal/taxon/772f78b1-f5e9-4851-853c-637d0fb3240b); [Mexico National Forestry Commission — Mexico City's old ahuehuetes](https://www.gob.mx/conafor/es/articulos/el-linaje-de-los-arboles-centenarios?idiom=es).

## 4. Antioquia, Colombia (Medellín)

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Coffee-country farmhouse, 15 × 10:** long single-story body of plastered earth/timber construction, with a moderate gabled or hipped clay-tile roof. Walls `#F0E6CE`, roof `#A15D43`, timber trim `#3E8079`. Distinctive detail: a shaded front veranda with two thin posts and a railing. |
| **Trees and plants — guadua bamboo** | **Guadua angustifolia, 8 × 16:** three slender green canes `#75954B` with dark nodes `#476438`; narrow, arching leaf sprays `#3E7447` form a loose upper crown. Native bamboo; useful along watercourses and field margins. |
| **Trees and plants — yellow trumpet tree** | **Guayacan, Handroanthus chrysanthus, 11 × 13:** gray-brown trunk `#786653`, a few thick ascending branches, and an open rounded crown `#63834B`. In dry spells it can shed leaves and carry conspicuous yellow flowers `#E6C44C`; avoid assigning a fixed temperate spring date. |
| **Trees and plants — arabica coffee** | **Coffea arabica, 7 × 7:** short brown stem `#71513B`, compact oval shrub with paired horizontal leaf tiers `#356647`. Small white blossom pixels `#EEE9D5` follow rain-triggered flowering; ripe berries `#B94D3F` appear later. Introduced African crop, emblematic of the cultivated slopes. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **Coffee solar dryer, 13 × 7:** low arched translucent cover `#D5DECE` on dark posts `#715A40`, over a flat ochre drying bed `#B99A61`. A typical working-farm feature rather than a unique regional monument. |

**Terrain:** steep green slopes, cultivated hillsides, deep valleys, and layered Andean ridges around the Aburrá Valley.

**Sources:** [Museum of Antioquia — regional domestic architecture](https://museodeantioquia.co/?coleccion=balcon); [Corantioquia — community plant nurseries](https://www.corantioquia.gov.co/red-de-bioviveros-comunitarios/); [Medellín Botanical Garden — yellow trumpet tree](https://www.botanicomedellin.org/vivenos/actualidad/ultimas-noticias/guayacanes-amarillos-que-embellecen-la-ciudad/); [Cenicafé — solar coffee dryers](https://publicaciones.cenicafe.org/index.php/avances_tecnicos/article/download/4247/4638).

## 5. Hudson Valley, United States (New York)

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Early regional stone farmhouse, 14 × 10:** long, low fieldstone body and a steep, straight gable roof. Stone walls `#969184`, shingle roof `#665F53`, door `#57473B`. Distinctive detail: a squat end chimney `#827668`. Gambrel roofs are also regional, but are not required for this older stone-house type. |
| **Trees and plants — sugar maple** | **Acer saccharum, 11 × 14:** straight brown trunk `#77604A`, dense rounded-to-oval crown `#66834B`. Autumn foliage `#DA9A3E` and `#BD5F36`; bare branching silhouette in winter. Native. |
| **Trees and plants — eastern white pine** | **Pinus strobus, 10 × 16:** tall straight trunk `#74604B`, layered horizontal branches and soft uneven foliage tiers `#476D59`. Evergreen; young trees are more conical than old trees. Native. |
| **Trees and plants — white oak** | **Quercus alba, 13 × 13:** stout gray trunk `#8A8275`, spreading limbs, broad lobed crown `#657448`. Autumn foliage muted red-brown `#956148`; winter branches exposed. Native. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **New World Dutch barn, 15 × 12:** broad, steep gable almost reaching the low sidewalls, weathered timber `#85715A`, roof `#625D53`; large paired wagon doors `#51463A` centered in the gable end. |

**Terrain:** river terraces and rolling farmland backed by wooded ridges, with the Catskills west of the middle valley and the Hudson Highlands farther south.

**Sources:** [Hudson River Valley National Heritage Area — architecture guide](https://www.hudsonrivervalley.com/documents/architecture1-pdf); [National Register — Krom Stone House and Dutch Barn](https://s3.amazonaws.com/NARAprodstorage/lz/electronic-records/rg-079/NPS_NY/95000955.pdf); [New York State Parks — Dutch heritage](https://parks.ny.gov/history/dutch-heritage); [New York environmental agency — Hudson habitat framework](https://extapps.dec.ny.gov/docs/remediation_hudson_pdf/hrebcf.pdf) and [native plant list](https://dec.ny.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/nynativeplantprioritylist.pdf).

## 6. Southern Sweden

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Scanian farmhouse, 15 × 10:** long, low half-timbered body with a steep thatched gable. Whitewashed infill `#E8E2CE`, dark timber `#51463A`, thatch `#99865A`. Distinctive detail: two vertical timber divisions and one diagonal brace. This selects Skåne's tradition within the broader southern region. |
| **Trees and plants — European beech** | **Fagus sylvatica, 12 × 14:** smooth pale-gray trunk `#A19D8D`, dense broad dome `#72934F`. Autumn crown copper `#B77F46`; winter branches mostly bare. Native to southern Sweden. |
| **Trees and plants — pedunculate oak** | **Quercus robur, 13 × 13:** short heavy trunk `#76634E`, crooked spreading limbs and a wide lobed crown `#627A47`. Autumn ochre-brown `#A18A4A`; winter branches exposed. Native. |
| **Trees and plants — European hornbeam** | **Carpinus betulus, 9 × 13:** slim gray trunk `#8E8D7D`, compact oval crown `#64864A`, narrowing toward the top. Autumn foliage yellow `#C4AF52`; winter crown largely bare. Native at Sweden's southern edge, documented at Stenshuvud. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **Traditional Scanian windmill, 11 × 16:** tapering dark wooden body `#625645`, small cap `#49463D`, and four pale lattice sails simplified to crossed bars `#C9BFA5`. Use a historic grain mill silhouette. |

**Terrain:** gently rolling agricultural plains, pasture and broadleaf woodland, with low ridges and coastal horizons in Skåne.

**Sources:** [Kulturen museum — Östarp farmstead](https://www.kulturen.com/welcome-kulturens-museums/kulturens-ostarp-2/); [University of Gothenburg — southern Swedish half-timbering](https://www.gu.se/sites/default/files/2024-11/Studier%20i%20sydsvenskt%20korsvirke%2C%20bibliografi.pdf); [Skåne County Administrative Board — Stenshuvud](https://www.lansstyrelsen.se/skane/besoksmal/nationalparker/stenshuvuds-nationalpark.html) and [traditional mills](https://www.lansstyrelsen.se/skane/besoksmal/kulturmiljoprogram/kulturmiljoprogram-skanes-historia-och-utveckling/kulturmiljoprogram-jordbrukets-landskap/de-skanska-mollorna.html).

## 7. Andalusia, Spain

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Cortijo farmhouse wing, 15 × 8:** long whitewashed masonry body with a shallow-to-moderate clay-tile gable; suggest the larger courtyard complex with one short adjoining wing. Walls `#F0E8D3`, roof `#AC6A4B`, door `#65513C`. Distinctive detail: a small square chimney `#DFD6BF`. |
| **Trees and plants — olive** | **Olea europaea, 10 × 9:** short twisted gray-brown trunk `#827664`, low rounded crown broken into two or three clumps `#8B9568`, silver highlights `#B0B394`. Emblematic cultivated tree; evergreen, often pruned into open orchard crowns. |
| **Trees and plants — holm oak** | **Quercus ilex subsp. rotundifolia, 12 × 11:** stout trunk `#78634B`, broad dense evergreen dome `#4E6444`. Use widely spaced trees for an oak pasture setting. |
| **Trees and plants — stone pine** | **Pinus pinea, 13 × 15:** tall bare trunk `#966F4D`, broad flattened umbrella crown `#416A4D` with a darker underside `#34553E`. Evergreen; emblematic in western sandy lowlands, including planted woodland. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **Olive-press counterweight tower, 6 × 12:** solid square masonry shaft `#DED3B8` rising above a low mill roof, with a small hipped tile cap `#A66A4B`. A historic olive-estate feature; keep its outline distinct from a church steeple. |

**Terrain:** rolling olive and cereal country, the broad Guadalquivir lowlands, and distant ranges; oak pasture and pine woodland belong to different local settings.

**Sources:** [Andalusian government — rural architecture inventory](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/organismos/viviendajuventudyordenaciondelterritorio/areas/arquitectura/fomento-arquitectura/paginas/cortijos-hacienda-lagares.html); [Málaga rural architecture volume — farmhouses and press towers](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/export/drupaljda/publicacion/12/05/cortijo_malaga.pdf); [Andalusian environmental portal — oak pasture and stone pine formations](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/medioambiente/portal/areas-tematicas/biodiversidad-y-vegetacion/caracterizacion-de-la-vegetacion-de-andalucia/formaciones-singulares).

## 8. São Paulo state, Brazil

| Element | Compact drawing reference |
| --- | --- |
| **Rural houses** | **Historic rural Paulista house, 15 × 9:** broad single-story rammed-earth body with a low-to-moderate hipped clay-tile roof. Limewashed walls `#E9DDC4`, roof `#A56243`, timber `#66503A`. Distinctive detail: a recessed central veranda, drawn as a dark rectangle beneath the continuous roof. Use the surviving bandeirista house type as a historical reference. |
| **Trees and plants — queen palm** | **Jeriva, Syagrus romanzoffiana, 9 × 16:** tall slim gray trunk `#9C9381`, loose crown of arching feather fronds `#4E8050`; one drooping fruit cluster `#D4A14E` is optional. Native palm; evergreen. |
| **Trees and plants — pink trumpet tree** | **Ipe, Handroanthus heptaphyllus, 11 × 13:** upright branching trunk `#806650`, rounded irregular crown `#63844A`. Pink-purple blossom masses `#BC78A3` on largely bare branches in the dry winter/early spring, roughly June–September. Native. |
| **Trees and plants — Brazilian pine** | **Araucaria angustifolia, 13 × 16:** tall straight trunk `#78634F`, sparse horizontal limbs and a flat, candelabra-like upper crown `#3D6650`. Evergreen; native to cooler upland forest, so reserve it for highland variants. |
| **Countryside landmark** | **Coffee-farm drying yard, 15 × 6 ground motif:** broad ochre terrace `#B88C60` bounded by a low retaining edge `#8D7360`, with a short tiled shed along one side, roof `#A56243`. A characteristic farm complex feature; give it ground space rather than treating it as a tall monument. |

**Terrain:** rolling inland plateaus and agricultural hills; higher terrain approaches the Mantiqueira and Serra do Mar ranges, with Atlantic Forest remnants.
