Research for a coming feature: regional countryside behind the train.

git-trains draws a pixel-art city behind a train. Between the tracks of its multi-track mode it also draws a countryside band: a village (small houses) and a forest (pines and round trees). Today those houses and trees look the same everywhere. The plan is to let a skyline file bring its region's countryside: a Japanese city with minka farmhouses and cherry trees, Guadalajara with adobe houses, blue agave and jacarandas, and so on.

Write `docs/research/regions.md`, in English, covering these eight regions:

1. Kansai, Japan (Osaka)
2. Jalisco, Mexico (Guadalajara)
3. Valley of Mexico (Mexico City)
4. Antioquia, Colombia (Medellín)
5. Hudson Valley, United States (New York)
6. Southern Sweden
7. Andalusia, Spain
8. São Paulo state, Brazil

For each region give:

- **Rural houses**: the traditional or typical countryside houses, described as silhouettes a pixel artist can draw in a box about 9 to 15 px wide and 6 to 12 px tall: roof shape and pitch, wall and roof colors (as hex), one distinctive detail (veranda, eaves, chimney, balcony...).
- **Trees and plants**: three or four native or emblematic ones, each as a silhouette in about 5 to 13 px wide and 6 to 16 px tall (shape, trunk, crown), with hex colors and, if relevant, the season when it looks different (blossom, autumn).
- **One landmark of the countryside** (a torii, a windmill, a hacienda chapel...), as a silhouette, if there is an obvious one.
- **Terrain**: what lies behind the countryside (mountains, sea, plains, hills), in one line.

Keep it factual and short: a table or a compact list per region. Name your sources at the end of each region (a few well-known references are enough). No logos, no brand names.
